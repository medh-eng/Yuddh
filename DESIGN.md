# YUDDH: Rise of the Rajyas — design

*What the game is, how it plays, and why it is built this way. Updated at the end of each session (last: 9 Nov 2026). Tasks live in `TASKS.md`; technical working notes for Claude live in `CLAUDE.md`.*

## Vision
A KARDS-style tactical card game of ancient Indian empires, for **web now and Google Play later (landscape only)**. History, not mythology: real armies with epic unit names (Rathi, Maharathi, Atirathi), no gods as units. Tone: a "living miniature": Indian miniature painting, each empire's card frame carved from its own material (Maurya sandstone, Gupta gold, Chalukya red sandstone, Chola bronze, Rajput silver).

Owner: Pon (directs the work, generates all art with AI image tools from prompts Claude writes). Live: https://medh-eng.github.io/Yuddh/ · repo: https://github.com/medh-eng/Yuddh (public).

## The rules in one page
- **Board:** each side has a **Rajya line** (up to 4 units) behind a shared **Yudhbhumi** (front, up to 4 units, held by one side at a time). Each side has a **Durg** (capital, 24 health). Destroy the enemy Durg to win.
- **Kosha** is the resource: +1 maximum every turn. Cards cost Kosha; advancing or attacking costs the unit's *order cost* (the bronze coin, usually 1).
- **Turn:** play cards, advance units from the Rajya line to the Yudhbhumi, attack. A unit either advances **or** attacks in a turn, except **charging classes** (Cavalry, Chariot, Elite Chariot, Legendary Warrior). Only units on the Yudhbhumi can strike the Durg; a **Raksha** unit on the defender's Rajya line guards the Durg and must fall first.
- **Melee trades blows** (both deal damage); **ranged** units strike without a counter-blow.
- **Promotion:** a unit that destroys an enemy and survives becomes Veer (+1 health), then Mahaveer (+1 attack, +1 health more).
- **Trample:** a War Elephant that crushes a unit carries the spare damage to that side's Durg.
- **Seasons (Ritu):** Sharad (none), Varsha (mud: nothing advances and attacks in one turn), Grishma (ranged deal 1 less), Hemanta (+1 Kosha each turn). Chosen by the battle's seed.
- **Dharma–Kuta meter** (−3..+3 under each Durg): fair melee kills +1 Dharma; hidden traps, poison and spies −1. At +3 the Durg strikes +1 harder; at −3 orders and Yantras cost 1 less.
- **Morale and rout:** a blow that leaves a unit at or below a third of its maximum health (rounded up), when it was above that line before, breaks it. It flees to the Rajya line (if there is room), takes no action in its owner's next turn, then rallies. A routed unit strikes no counter-blow, and cutting it down earns no Dharma. An attacker that is broken by the counter-blow breaks too, unless it destroyed its foe. **Never broken:** Raksha shield-walls, Legendary Warriors, Mahaveer (twice-honoured) units, every unit of an army whose standard-bearer (Raja Dhwaja) still stands, and every unit of an army at full Dharma (+3). All numbers are in `rules.json` ("morale").
- **Yantras** are face-down traps that spring on their own trigger (when the enemy deploys, attacks your Durg, or attacks your unit).
- **River boats** (Sindhu Nauka): up to two ready Rajya-line units cross to the Yudhbhumi free and may strike that turn.
- **Pikes** (Seleucid Phalanx): a melee unit that advanced this turn and strikes it takes 2 extra damage.
- Two rulesets exist: `'classic'` (the original prototype, replayed by the golden test) and `'yuddh'` (everything above, and all new work). New rule changes go behind `st.rules === 'yuddh'`.

## The enemy's mind (AI levels)
- **easy / normal / hard:** the prototype's heuristics (golden-tested on the classic rules), made morale-aware under the Yuddh rules. Every battle is balanced against **normal** playing both sides.
- **search (Veteran foe):** `src/engine/search.ts`. Each turn it beam-searches its own sequences of plays, advances and attacks on copies of the board; unseen cards (the player's hand, hidden Yantras, its own deck order) are re-dealt at random so it never reads hidden information; the best finished turns are tested against the player's reply (the careful 'hard' heuristics, on three different re-deals) and it plays the first part of the best plan, planning again whenever the real game shows something it could not know. About 10 ms a turn. It beats the normal AI about 85% of the time over both seats (tests/search.test.ts). Sizes are in `rules.json` ("ai").
- The player chooses the foe on every briefing: **Foe: Standard** (default) or **Foe: Veteran** (one setting for the whole game, `src/campaign/difficulty.ts`). The tutorial is always standard.

## Architecture
- **Engine** (`src/engine/`): pure TypeScript, no DOM. Rules, AI, events. Runs in browser and node.
- **Data** (`src/data/`): *all balance lives in JSON.* `cards.json` (every card and empire), `decks.json` (the decks of each battle), `rules.json` (ranks, seasons, Dharma limit, charge classes, Kuta cards). A balance change is an edit there, never a code rewrite. Order effects that fit common patterns are data-driven too (`power`, `hqDamage`, `draw`, `dharma`, `reprisal` fields); a new *kind* of effect needs a few lines in `rules.ts`.
- **Campaign** (`src/campaign/`): scenario definitions (story text, Durgs, AI level, setup) + `campaign.ts` (order, progress). Decks come from `decks.json`.
- **Renderer** (`src/game/`): PixiJS 8 + GSAP; two screen modes (desktop 1920×1080 design space; phone landscape 720-high). Scenes: title, campaign map, battle, card gallery, preview.
- **Tests** (`tests/`): golden traces (154 games from the prototype, never break), rules, card mechanics, battle balance (AI vs AI win-rate bands), scripted real-mouse playtests in `tests/e2e/`.

## Rank and renown: the Roll of Renown (built 9 Nov 2026)
Each empire's campaign earns **Yash** (renown) on its own, and Yash sets a rank on a seven-step ladder named after the ancient Indian army: **Padati** (foot soldier) → **Gulmapati** (captain) → **Nayaka** (major) → **Dandanayaka** (colonel) → **Senapati** (general) → **Mahasenapati** (commander-in-chief) → **Chakravartin** (emperor). Yash: +80 for a win, +120 more for the first win in a battle, +20 for a defeat, ×1.5 against the Veteran foe; thresholds 0, 150, 400, 800, 1400, 2200, 3200 (`src/data/ranks.json`; code in `src/meta/standing.ts`, saved in localStorage `yuddh.standing.v1`). The page (title → "Rank & Renown", `scenes/standing.ts`) shows one column per empire: rank (English, Hindi, plain gloss), progress bar, Yash to the next rank, battles cleared, wins, Veteran wins; tapping a column opens that campaign. The end-of-battle screen shows "+N Yash" and "RANK UP". A rank-up pays Kirti (25 per rank), which will matter when the collection and deck builder arrive (Phase 4).
**Rule of the design: ranks never change a battle.** No stats, no extra cards, no purchasable currency: the balance bands (45–70%) stay true for everyone.
**Rewards, in order of effort (proposed, not built; Pon picks):** (1) the title and a small badge beside the empire's name on the briefing and victory screens; (2) at Nayaka, the empire's "war council" page in the History panel: a longer chronicle of that empire; (3) at Senapati, a gilded card frame and card back for that empire's cards in battle and the gallery; (4) at Chakravartin, a painted victory banner and a remixed theme piece for that empire.

## Two-human play (idea from Pon, 10 Nov 2026; steps 1 and 2 BUILT 10 Nov)
Goal: two people play each other, on one phone (pass-and-play) or on two phones.
- **The engine already suits it.** It is deterministic (seeded rng plus a list of actions), has no DOM code, and an action is a small JSON object. Two devices that start from the same seed and apply the same actions stay identical, so only actions need to travel ("lockstep"). Hidden hands are a display matter; a friendly duel does not need server-side secrecy (a ranked mode later would).
- **Step 1, pass-and-play (one device), built:** title → "Two players" → Player 1 picks a faction, Player 2 picks (16 factions in `src/data/duel.json`, any pairing incl. mirror) → battle. How it works: `engine/duel.ts` `swapSides` turns the state round after each turn so the mover is always player 0 at the bottom (a swapped game matches a plain one, tests/duel.test.ts); `fixTurnAfterEnd` keeps the turn counter right; the battle scene (`duel` / `flipped`, `duelHandOver`, `drawCurtain`, `duelEndScreen`) hides the board behind a "pass the device" curtain between turns; Player 2 draws one extra card; the season is random; no Yash or campaign progress. Original plan notes:
- **Step 1 plan (original).** New "Duel" mode: pick two decks (e.g. Rajput vs Ghurid, any two campaign decks), no AI. Needs: (a) a `viewer` index in `scenes/battle.ts`, which hard-codes player 0 as the human and the bottom row at about 15 places (`st.active === 0`, hand, row choice per owner); (b) a "pass the phone to <side>" curtain between turns so hands stay hidden; (c) a deck-pick screen; (d) no Yash or campaign progress from duels. About one session.
- **Step 2, two phones: BUILT (10 Nov night).** Title → "Two phones" → pick your own faction → Host or Join (`net/pairing.ts`, a DOM overlay): the host shows a code (QR + text), the guest scans or pastes it and shows a reply code, the host takes that, and the phones talk directly over a WebRTC data channel (`net/link.ts`; codes are deflate-raw + base64url, prefix `Y1.`; STUN stun.l.google.com, override with `window.__yuddhIce`; tests use `[]`). Then the guest says hello with its faction and the host sends `{seed, factions, ground}`. Lockstep: both run the engine from the seed and exchange only actions with a `syncHash` per action; the host keeps the real frame, the guest's state is kept swapped (`swapSides` at start) so the local player is always player 0 on screen; actions cross the wire in the host's frame (`mirrorAction`); turn counter fixed up (+1 after the guest's own end, −1 after the host's end applied on the guest). In `battle.ts`: `online`, `netSend`, `pump`/`applyRemote` (replays the other player's action with the AI-turn animation path), `netLost` (disconnect or desync screen → title). **Connection hardening (11 Nov, after Pon's first real-phone test failed on mobile data):** (1) `slim()` used to drop every IPv6 candidate and keep the first four lines, which on an IPv6-first Indian mobile network (Jio, Airtel) or a phone with several interfaces could remove the only usable address: it now keeps up to 2 host, 3 server-reflexive (public) and 2 relay UDP candidates, public IPv6 included, ordered host, public, relay; (2) ICE servers: three public STUN servers plus a best-effort public TURN relay (Open Relay, `DEFAULT_ICE` in link.ts; untested from the sandbox, may be down); (3) the QR was drawn about 130 px wide and was unreadable on a phone camera: it is now 210-420 px, rendered at 720 px, tap to fill the screen; scanning uses the built-in BarcodeDetector where it exists (Chrome on Android) and jsQR otherwise, at up to 1080p; (4) the pairing overlay's top was clipped on short screens (flex centring); (5) a failed connection now says the ICE state and how many local / public / relay / IPv6 candidates the code carried, and suggests the same WiFi or a hotspot. Still no hidden hands, no reconnect. Original limits: hands are not secret (a player could read them from the browser console), STUN only (no relay: strict firewalls / symmetric NAT may fail; mDNS candidates can fail on some networks), no reconnect, no rematch online. Tests: tests/net.test.ts (codes), tests/duel.test.ts (lockstep hash), tests/e2e/online.cjs (two browser pages, paste route).
- **Step 2 original plan: two phones, same room or online.** A web page cannot do phone-to-phone Bluetooth (Web Bluetooth only talks to peripherals and is missing on iPhone). Use **WebRTC peer-to-peer**, paired by QR code or a short code, works over the same Wi-Fi or a hotspot, no game server. Reuses step 1's viewer and sends actions over the data channel.
- **Step 3, real Bluetooth / Wi-Fi Direct.** Only once the game is wrapped as an Android app for Google Play (Capacitor): the Nearby Connections plugin gives offline phone-to-phone play. The same action channel is reused.

## Google Play: the Android app (13 Nov 2026)
The game is wrapped with **Capacitor** (the route step 3 above already chose): `capacitor.config.ts` (app id **com.medheng.yuddh** — permanent once the first AAB reaches Play, changeable until then), `android/` (landscape-only `sensorLandscape`, immersive-sticky fullscreen + keep-screen-on in MainActivity, CAMERA permission for the QR scan, adaptive icons generated from `resources/` by `@capacitor/assets`). The whole `dist/` build (~91 MB, well under Play's 200 MB AAB limit) ships inside the app, so single-player and pass-and-play work fully **offline**; two-phone play still needs internet for pairing. The sandbox cannot reach Google's SDK servers, so the app is built by **GitHub Actions** (`.github/workflows/android.yml`, run from the Actions tab): it always produces `yuddh-debug.apk` (install directly on any phone), and when the repo has the four signing secrets (`KEYSTORE_BASE64`, `KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `KEY_ALIAS`, `KEY_PASSWORD`) it also produces a signed `yuddh-release.apk` + `yuddh-release.aab` for the Play Console. `versionCode` comes from the workflow run number, so every build can be uploaded over the last. The upload keystore itself is NOT in the repo (public!) — it was generated 13 Nov and handed to Pon in chat to store as those secrets and keep safe. Play Store checklist for Pon: a Play Console developer account (one-time $25), the AAB from the workflow, store listing (screenshots, descriptions — the gallery and battles screenshot beautifully), a privacy policy URL (the game stores everything locally; a one-page note on mayflowerclinic.in or GitHub Pages is enough), content rating questionnaire, and Play App Signing on (default; Google holds the final key, our keystore is only the upload key, so even if lost it can be reset).

## Duel balance (11 Nov 2026)
Pon: pass-and-play Maurya against Gupta was lopsided. Cause (measured, AI-vs-AI "normal", every faction against every other, both seats): a duel runs about 16 turns and both sides draw every turn, but the campaign decks hold 20-31 cards, so decks ran dry and **attrition** (a growing Durg wound per empty draw, `fatigue`) decided most games; Gupta, whose Mantras each draw a card, lost 94% of its games that way (Maurya 92% vs Gupta 8%). Fixes: (1) every duel deck is padded to **52 cards** (`DUEL_DECK_SIZE` in `campaign/duel.ts`) by repeating its non-legendary cards in order; (2) each faction in `data/duel.json` has an optional `hq` (Durg hit points; the main lever, 14-44) and `kosha` (starting Kosha ceiling; Gupta has 3, with hq 24), tuned by simulation. Result: Maurya vs Gupta ~50/50 from either seat. **Not equal yet:** the other factions still range from about 28% to 77% average win rate, because some campaign decks are simply stronger (Chalukya, Ghurid, Rajput, Vardhana strong; Forest, Sinhala, Satrap weak) and Durg hit points cannot offset a whole deck. The second player's extra card still favours the second seat (first seat wins about 40-45%). Next ideas: dedicated duel decks (one per faction, built to a cost curve), a coin toss, per-pairing handicaps shown on the picker. `tests/duelBalance.test.ts` guards padding and the Maurya-Gupta pair.

## Duel balance, round 2: add/trim deck tuning (13 Nov 2026)
Since Durg hit points could not offset a whole deck (the strongest factions won ~65-76% even at 12 hq, the weakest lost at 60), the real lever from the balance lesson — card counts — was made data-driven: `duel.json` factions now take an optional **`add`** (extra copies appended to the campaign deck) and **`trim`** (one copy removed per listed id), applied *before* the 52-card padding so the padding repeats the tuned deck and a single edit carries weight. Neither touches `decks.json`, so campaign balance is untouched. The lists were found by a greedy simulated search (every ordered pair, both seats, AI 'normal'): for each outlier faction, try one edit per distinct card, keep the one that moves its field-wide win rate closest to 50%, repeat. Result: the field tightened from **21-76%** to **36-61%** (N=20 per pair, 800 games per faction). Patterns the search found on its own: elephants are what weak decks lack (Forest, Sinhala, Naga, Nanda all got gaja copies; trample wins duels) and what strong decks lose (Chalukya, Western Chalukya, Pallava Badami all trim elephants). Forest (~37%) and Pallava (~61%) are the last outliers: acceptable for a casual mode, and the next real step remains dedicated duel decks per faction. `tests/duelBalance.test.ts` now also sweeps every ordered pair (N=4) and fails if any faction leaves 22-78%. Tuning scripts' recipe: loop ordered pairs with `duelGame(a,b,seed)` + `aiTurn(st, st.active, 'normal')` — a full 420-pair matrix at N=16 takes ~5 s under vite-node, so re-tuning after deck changes is cheap. Beware the field effect: a buff to one faction shifts every other faction's measured rate, so tune from a saved baseline, in whole-field passes, not faction by faction in sequence.
