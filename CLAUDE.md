# YUDDH: Rise of the Rajyas — working notes for Claude

KARDS-style card game of ancient India, built for Pon (owner, non-programmer, directs the work and generates all art with AI image tools from prompts Claude writes). Target: web now, **Google Play** later (landscape only). Repo is public; live at https://medh-eng.github.io/Yuddh/.

## Ground rules
- **Never break the golden test.** `tests/golden.test.ts` replays 154 games recorded from Sarwam's prototype engine. If a rule is changed on purpose, re-record with `npm run golden` and say so in the commit.
- The engine (`src/engine/`) has **no DOM code**. All screen work goes in `src/game/`.
- **Two rulesets.** `newGame({ rules })`: `'classic'` (default) is the prototype's rules, which the golden traces replay; `'yuddh'` (The First Throne and everything new) adds: a Raksha on the defender's Rajya line guards the Durg, and a unit either advances or attacks in a turn, except the charging classes (Cavalry, Chariot, Elite Chariot, Legendary Warrior → `canCharge`), which may advance then strike. **Promotion** (Yuddh only): a unit that destroys an enemy in its own attack and survives becomes Veer (+1 health), then Mahaveer (+1 attack, +1 health more); `unit.rank`, event `promote`, gold stars on the token. **Trample**: a War Elephant that destroys a unit carries the unneeded damage to that side's Durg (event `trample`). **Seasons** (`st.season`, `SEASONS` in rules.ts): Sharad (none), Varsha (mud: nothing advances and attacks in one turn), Grishma (heat haze: ranged deal 1 less, `strikeDamage()`), Hemanta (+1 Kosha each turn). The First Throne picks the season from its seed (`seasonFor`); the board shows a tappable season label and weather particles. **Batch 3 cards** (yuddhCards.ts): deploys kosha2 (Dhana Nanda), setYantra (Amatya Rakshasa sets a free Pratikar), hillVolley (Parvataka), elephantBuff (Hastipaka); orders nd_nanda_kosha (+3 Kosha), m_garuda_vyuha (+1 atk and `unit.surge = turn`: may advance and attack), m_makara_vyuha (+2 health on the Yudhbhumi); m_raja_dhwaja morale (+1 damage to comrades on the Yudhbhumi, inside `strikeDamage`); m_shalya_vaidya heals 2 at turn start (event `heal`); nd_kashtha_prakara ward 3 + 2 back at the attacker; trample applies to any `isElephant` sub. **Dharma–Kuta** (`player.dharma`, −3..+3, unset = 0, `dharmaOf`): a melee unit destroying an enemy in its own attack +1; setting a Yantra (incl. Amatya Rakshasa's) or playing n_visha_baan / m_gudhchar −1 (event `dharma`). At +3 Durg strikes +1; at −3 orders and Yantras cost 1 less (`koshaCost`). Meters under each Durg, tappable; the tutorial has a step on it. New cards: add to cards.json "yuddh", never "classic". Put new rule changes behind `st.rules === 'yuddh'` so the golden test stays meaningful.
- Before pushing: `npm test`, `npx tsc --noEmit`, `npm run build`, then a real playtest: `npm run preview` + `node tests/e2e/playtest.cjs http://localhost:4173/ <outdir> [w h]` (set `PW=/opt/npm-tools/node_modules/playwright CHROME=/usr/bin/google-chrome` in the cloud sandbox) and look at the screenshots. Test desktop and phone landscape (844×390 or 732×412), one at a time (two at once starve the headless GPU and drags time out). The playtest visits the gallery, plays a whole battle, films an enemy turn (13-enemy-turn-*), and lists any drag that had no effect with a `fail-N.png`. The sandbox renders 1600×900 at ~4 fps or worse (2 cores, software GL); when frames pass 0.5 s GSAP's lag smoothing slows every tween to a crawl and the board looks frozen mid-fade, so run the desktop playtest at **1280×720** (still desktop mode). The bot sets `window.__yuddhLongPressMs = 4000` to stop the 0.48 s hold-to-read firing mid-drag. With a mouse the hovered hand card lifts over its neighbours, so the bot steps off the hand before each grab and aims at a card's resting place (`home`). A desktop game takes ~10 minutes: run it in the background with a 25-minute limit. Any failure is now a bug.
- Waiting on a background process: never `pgrep -f` a pattern that also appears in your own shell's command line (it matches itself forever).
- Never `pkill -f "vite preview"` from a shell whose own command line contains that text (kills the shell); start servers with `setsid`.
- GitHub Pages settings API is blocked from the sandbox; publishing is the `deploy.yml` workflow pushing `dist/` to `gh-pages`.
- Commit as "Claude", attribution lines per session instructions.
- **Standing conventions (Pon, 7 Nov 2026):** (1) keep `DESIGN.md` and `TASKS.md` in the repo and update both at the end of every session; (2) game data (units, ranks, abilities, decks, season rules) lives in `src/data/*.json`, never hard-coded: balance changes are tiny JSON edits; (3) `README.md` has the file-layout guide for AI agents: keep it current when files move.

- **Morale / rout** (Yuddh only, `checkRout`, `wouldRout`, `isSteadfast`, `routLine` in rules.ts; data in rules.json "morale"): `unit.routed` 1 = just broke (it loses its owner's next turn), 2 = resting, cleared at the following turn start (events `rout`, `rally`). A broken unit on the Yudhbhumi flees to the Rajya line if there is room. Routed units strike no counter-blow and give no Dharma when killed. Never broken: Raksha, Legendary Warrior, Mahaveer, an army with Raja Dhwaja standing, an army at +3 Dharma. The AI knows (`attackScore`).
- **Search AI** (`search.ts`, `aiLevel: 'search'`): `planTurn` clones the state (`cloneState`: JSON copy, own rng seeded from the position), re-deals hidden cards (`redeal`: sorted first, so the real arrangement cannot leak), beam-searches, tests finalists against 'hard' replies. **It clears `actionObservers` while it thinks** (the battle's replay observer would otherwise snapshot the imaginary games) and restores them in `finally`; the real moves are then played with `applyAction`. `applyActionCore` is the silent version. The player picks Standard/Veteran on the briefing (`difficulty.ts`, localStorage `yuddh.foe.v1`).

## Stack
TypeScript, Vite 8, PixiJS 8, GSAP 3, Vitest. Fonts bundled via @fontsource (theme.ts): **Cinzel** (`FONT_TITLE`) for titles, names, labels and every number; **Alegreya Sans** (`FONT_BODY`) for rules text, panels, hints and coaching; Cormorant Garamond (`FONT_SERIF`) only for story lines (briefing, end-screen line); Tiro Devanagari Hindi. Cinzel is wide: anything that can grow (names, banners, info) shrinks to fit. Two screen modes (`src/game/view.ts`): desktop uses a 1920×1080 design space; a phone held sideways (short side ≤ 540 CSS px) gets a 720-high design space 1280–1800 wide (follows the screen's shape, 16:9 to 2.5:1), plus `view.ui` (1.25) for text and controls. Phone layout is tuned for 5.5–6 inch phones: test at 640×360 and 732×412 as well as 844×390. Responsive scenes read `view` and implement `relayout()`; the battle keeps its numbers in one `battleLayout()` table per mode and rebuilds the board from the game state when the mode changes. A scene can pin a fixed size with `design` (the old AI-vs-AI preview does). The battlefield fills the window behind everything (`Stage.setBackdrop`).

## Hard-won lessons
- **Kill tweens before destroying display objects.** A GSAP tween on a destroyed Pixi object throws every frame and freezes all animation. Use `destroyTree()` for containers; `TokenView`, `HandCard`, `DurgView` already stop their own tweens.
- The battle replays the AI turn by subscribing to `actionObservers` and snapshotting state per action; `reconcile(state)` makes the screen match a state (damage numbers come from hp diffs).
- `window.__yuddh` exposes read-only state and `moves()` (every legal drag) for the e2e bot.
- Art masters arrive on GitHub (Pon uploads via the web UI) in `art/source/`; run `npm run assets` and commit the outputs.
- Card art aspect ratios vary (1024×1536, 1060×1484…); the painter crops with a focus point, so keep the full picture.
- **Decoration inside an interactive scene must have `eventMode = 'none'`.** The battle scene listens for taps everywhere (`static`), and Pixi then hit-tests every descendant as if interactive: a passing spark, the turn banner or a fading card silently swallowed presses on the unit beneath it. The battle's lanes, arrow, preview and fx layers opt out.
- Faces painted before their painting has loaded are drawn with a placeholder and not cached; load a scene's art (loadArt) before building it.
- Cards drawn from the deck are face-down and `flipping` while they fly in; the battle reports itself busy (`dealing()`) until they land.
- Hand cards show their current cost (`HandCard.setCost(koshaCost(...))` in refreshInteractivity); a discount (Maurya passive, upgrades) paints the coin green. Card faces mark charging classes "· CHARGES".
- Tapping an enemy unit reads its card; a tap that just chose a target is ignored for 0.7 s (`targetTapAt`).
- The playtest presses a hand card on its uncovered left strip (cards overlap on narrow phones).
- Board units (`TokenView`): badges sit inside the frame's lower corners; on phones the painting fills the token (no name plate, `tokenFace(id, owner, false)`) and the name shows above it while selected. The bronze order-cost coin (with a sword mark) appears only when the cost isn't 1. Lines keep ≥24 px between units (`lineFit`/`slotX`, inner `body` scale via `setFit`). Keywords in card text are bold bronze (`wrapRich`, KEYWORD regex in paint.ts).
- Layer order in battle: hand, arrow, damage shields, reading panels and fx are re-added above the meters and season chip; tips and the coach layer go above those.
- Fullscreen: `src/game/fullscreen.ts`; the second round button in battle; a phone's first tap goes fullscreen once (main.ts); `public/manifest.webmanifest` (landscape, fullscreen).
- Combat feedback (KARDS-like): while aiming a unit, red damage shields show what each side would take (`combatFx`, above the arrow); hits pop a red shield with the number, the struck unit flashes red and flinches, melee raises dust.
- **Paintings load defensively (manifest.ts).** Max 6 requests at once, each retried 5× with a pause, a 15 s stall guard, `decode()` before use. Screens never wait for them for long (`orTimeout`: map 4 s, battle 20 s). A face painted with the placeholder glyph is repainted in place when its painting arrives (`healLater` in paint.ts via `whenArt`). Cause of the 7 Nov report (some cards showing only a glyph, a blank campaign map): one failed or stalled request used to be swallowed for good.
- Music needs a user gesture; `audio.playMusic()` before unlock just records the wish. `audio.unlock()` runs on every pointerdown and retries a blocked play.
