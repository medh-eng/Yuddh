# YUDDH art prompts: batch 3 (Phase 2)

Twelve card paintings for Phase 2: the Nanda court gets its own faces (today the Nandas borrow neutral soldiers), and the Maurya side gets the heroes and formations that the new rules need.

**Order of work:** the six Nanda cards first, because they change The First Throne straight away. Then the six Maurya cards.

## Same rules as before

- Paste the **Maurya and neutral era style block** (from `PROMPTS-BATCH2.md`) first, then the card prompt. All twelve cards are set around 320 BCE.
- Portrait 5:7, as large as the tool allows. Add *"full figure with clear space above the head and below the feet"* for people.
- No text, borders or watermarks.
- Name each file exactly as listed and upload it to `art/source/cards/`.

---

## The Nanda court (6)

**`nd_dhana_nanda.png` — Dhana Nanda, the last Nanda king**
```
The last Nanda king of Magadha seated on a low throne heaped with gold coins and chests of treasure, heavy and proud, thick gold collar and earrings, a jewelled turban, one hand resting on a sack of coins, cold suspicious eyes. Attendants with fly-whisks half-hidden in shadow behind him. Rich crimson and gold, the feel of great wealth and little love.
```

**`nd_amatya_rakshasa.png` — Amatya Rakshasa, the Nanda minister**
```
An older statesman of the Nanda court, lean and watchful, grey beard, plain white uttariya over one shoulder, holding a signet ring up to the lamplight, a scroll in the other hand. Behind him a carved wooden pillar of a palace hall. Clever, loyal and dangerous: Chanakya's great rival.
```

**`nd_gaja_sena.png` — Nanda elephant column**
```
A column of war elephants of the Nanda army advancing toward the viewer through dust, the lead elephant large in the foreground with a red and gold caparison, bells and tusk rings, a mahout on its neck and two archers in a howdah, more elephants fading into the dust behind. Overwhelming weight and noise.
```

**`nd_nanda_kosha.png` — The Nanda treasury (an order card)**
```
The inside of a royal treasury vault lit by oil lamps: stacked chests overflowing with punch-marked silver and gold coins, a scribe counting on a tablet, a guard with a spear at the door. No single hero: the treasure itself is the subject, glowing in the lamplight.
```

**`nd_ratha_dal.png` — Nanda chariot squadron**
```
A squadron of four-horse war chariots of the Nanda army charging across a dusty plain, the nearest chariot large in the foreground with a driver and an archer drawing a bow, parasol-topped poles, red pennants streaming, wheels throwing up dust, more chariots behind.
```

**`nd_kashtha_prakara.png` — The timber walls of Pataliputra (a Yantra card)**
```
A great wall of heavy timber stakes with wooden watch towers and loopholes for archers, built along a riverbank, a moat of dark water in front, guards on the walkway at dusk with torches. Seen from the outside, looking up at it: forbidding and strong.
```

## Maurya heroes and formations (6)

**`m_parvataka.png` — Parvataka, the mountain king (Chandragupta's ally)**
```
A rugged king from the Himalayan foothills, broad-shouldered, wolf-fur mantle over a cotton tunic, a long straight sword at his hip, snow-capped peaks and pine forest behind him, his hill warriors with spears in the background. Proud, bold, not entirely to be trusted.
```

**`m_garuda_vyuha.png` — Garuda Vyuha, the eagle formation (an order card)**
```
An army drawn up in the shape of a giant eagle with outstretched wings, seen from high above a wide plain: blocks of infantry form the body, cavalry the two wings, chariots the beak, elephants the tail. Faint golden lines trace the shape of the eagle over the troops, like a diagram on a battle map.
```

**`m_makara_vyuha.png` — Makara Vyuha, the crocodile formation (an order card)**
```
An army drawn up in the shape of a makara, the Indian crocodile-sea monster, seen from high above a plain by a river: elephants form the long snout and jaws, infantry the body, cavalry the curling tail. Faint golden lines trace the makara's outline over the troops.
```

**`m_raja_dhwaja.png` — The royal standard-bearer**
```
A tall young warrior holding high the Maurya royal standard, a long saffron banner with a golden emblem of a lion streaming in the wind, his other hand raised as he shouts to the troops, soldiers behind him lifting their spears in answer. Courage and morale, the moment before the charge.
```

**`m_shalya_vaidya.png` — The army surgeon**
```
An army surgeon of ancient India kneeling beside a wounded soldier in a field camp, cleaning and binding a wound with clean cloth, a leather roll of fine bronze surgical instruments open beside him, small pots of herbs and oil, a lamp. Calm, skilled hands, kind face. The Arthashastra says surgeons with instruments and medicines marched behind the army.
```

**`m_hastipaka.png` — The mahout (elephant master)**
```
A wiry elephant master sitting on the neck of a great war elephant, holding an ankusha (elephant goad) and speaking softly into the elephant's ear, the elephant's eye calm and intelligent, decorated with a simple cloth and bells. Close and personal: the bond between the man and the animal.
```
