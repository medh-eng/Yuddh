# YUDDH art prompts: batch 19, "Vikramaditya's Return" (Chalukya campaign, battle 4, c. 654–655 CE)

The Fall of Badami does not end the Chalukya story. **Vikramaditya I**, Pulakeshin II's son, spent the years after 642 CE rebuilding his father's shattered power from whatever ground the Pallava had not taken. His own copper-plate grants (the Gadval, Nerur and Kendur plates, issued across his reign) say he "restored the fortunes of his family" and drove the Pallava out of Vatapi. Most historians place the recapture of the capital at around **654–655 CE**, with the wider Chalukya revival continuing through the 660s and 670s. **This battle is the reconquest of Vatapi itself** — the last step of a long rebuilding, not the whole of it.

**Why this battle:** Badami was a loss the game let you share honestly. This is the answer, and it closes the arc that began at the Narmada: a kingdom broken at its own gate, and a son who takes it back. Reusing the Vatapi battlefield (already painted) makes the point without a word of text — same cliffs, same lake, the pieces on the board simply changed sides.

Facts the game keeps honest (the History note will say these):
- **No named Pallava commander for this fight.** Narasimhavarman I (the Pallava king from the Fall of Badami) did not die until around 668 CE, well after Vatapi fell back to the Chalukyas, so the man Vikramaditya actually beat on the ground was a **garrison or governor, not named in any surviving source**. The game invents no name for him and calls the enemy simply "the Pallava garrison of Vatapi."
- **The date is a historians' estimate** (c. 654–655 CE), built from the gap between Vikramaditya I's accession (c. 655 CE, itself approximate) and later Chalukya inscriptions that already treat Vatapi as recovered. **No surviving record describes the recapture battle itself** — it is reconstructed entirely from before-and-after facts. The whole fight, the cards, and the order of events are therefore **INVENTED** from that gap, same as Pullalur and Badami before it.
- **The Pillar of Victory is real** (the Pallava inscription on it survives, now in a Badami museum) and so is the idea of the Chalukyas wanting it back, but no source says what, if anything, they did to it after retaking the city. The game does not invent an answer to that.
- **Era (c. 650–670 CE):** the same clothing and equipment as Narmada, Pullalur and Badami — nothing changes in one generation. Chalukya in their established look (dhotis, turbans, the boar/Varaha banner, red sandstone). The Pallava garrison in the established Pallava look (the clothing sentence below), but worn and dug-in rather than fresh from the march.

This batch is **8 images, no new battlefield** (reuse `vatapi.png`): 1 Durg, 3 Chalukya cards, 4 Pallava cards.

## Same rules as before

- Cards portrait 5:7, as large as the tool allows. Durg square. No text, borders or watermarks. Add "full figure with clear space above the head and below the feet" for single-person cards.
- **Chalukya cards**: red sandstone Deccan cliffs, the boar (Varaha) banner, dhotis, turbans, round shields and spears — the same look as `c_pulakeshin.png`, `c_rakshak.png` and the rest of the Badami set.
- **Pallava cards and Durg**: use this clothing sentence in every prompt, same as batch 18: *"South Indian early seventh century dress: a white or ochre dhoti with a sash, gold armlets and a tall conical crown for a king or governor, soldiers in short quilted coats or light mail, round shields, spears and bows, a lion or bull (Nandi) banner."* These troops have held the city for twelve years: worn gear, patched banners, a garrison rather than a field army.
- Upload cards and the Durg to `art/source/cards/`. **Please check the folder after uploading** — no battlefield in this batch, so nothing should land in `art/source/battlefields/` this time.

## After the pictures arrive

Claude builds **Vikramaditya's Return** as Chalukya battle 4 (Narmada → Pullalur → Badami → Vikramaditya's Return), reusing the `vatapi` battlefield and the `durg_badami` Durg (now the player's again). You play **Vikramaditya I** retaking the capital; the enemy is the unnamed Pallava garrison. Planned cards: Vikramaditya I (legendary), Vatapi Vanguard, Punarasthapana (Kosha/Durg restore); Pallava Governor of Vatapi, Garrison of Vatapi (Raksha), Guardians of the Pillar (Raksha), The Siege Holds (order). Existing Chalukya and neutral cards from Badami are reused where they fit. The battle is meant to feel like a hard-won recovery rather than a rout — somewhere around 50–55% for the Chalukya side, a shade kinder than Badami's 45% since this is the win the history actually gives them. Claude adds a History note (c. 654–655 CE; the Gadval/Nerur/Kendur plates for Vikramaditya's own claim; the "no named Pallava commander" and "date is an estimate from a gap" caveats above; an epilogue noting the Chalukya–Pallava war still had generations left to run), balance tests, and a playtest. This closes the Chalukya campaign's four-battle arc; a fifth battle (further Chalukya–Pallava or Chalukya–Pandya fighting later in Vikramaditya I's reign) is possible later but not planned yet.

**Not in this batch (optional, any time):** the six Rajput regalia medallions (`rg_rajput_1` to `6`); batch 17 (Koppam, Chola battle 4) is still waiting for its paintings, which are not in the repository yet.
